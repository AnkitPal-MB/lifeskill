# Listening and Active Communication

## 1. Active Listening

### Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?

-   Give the speaker your full attention.
-   Avoid distractions and stop thinking about your reply.
-   Do not interrupt; let the person finish.
-   Use eye contact and simple body language to show interest.
-   Use small responses like "I see" or "okay" to encourage them.
-   Ask questions when something is not clear.
-   Paraphrase the main point in your own words.
-   Take notes when the information is important.

## 2. Reflective Listening

### Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?

-   Focus completely on the conversation and remove distractions.
-   Try to understand the speaker's point of view without judging them.
-   Do not assume that understanding someone means agreeing with them.
-   Listen to both the words and the emotions behind them.
-   Repeat the main idea in your own words to check your understanding.
-   Ask for clarification if something is unclear.
-   Confirm that your understanding matches what the speaker actually
    meant.
-   Show empathy so the speaker feels comfortable sharing more.

## 3. Reflection

### Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

-   I sometimes get distracted by my phone or surroundings.
-   I start thinking about my reply before the person finishes.
-   I sometimes make assumptions instead of listening fully.
-   Long conversations can make me lose focus.
-   Stress or tiredness can reduce my attention.
-   Sometimes I focus more on the words than the speaker's feelings.

### Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

-   Keep my phone away during important conversations.
-   Focus on the speaker instead of preparing my reply.
-   Let the person finish before I respond.
-   Ask questions instead of making assumptions.
-   Paraphrase important points to confirm my understanding.
-   Take notes during meetings and technical discussions.
-   Pay attention to tone, facial expressions and body language.
-   Practice listening without judging or interrupting.

## 4. Types of Communication

### Question 5: When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?

-   When I do not want to hurt someone's feelings.
-   When I am unsure about my opinion.
-   When I avoid saying "no" to someone.
-   When I let others make a decision even when I have a different
    opinion.
-   When I avoid an argument even though I should speak up.

### Question 6: When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

-   When I am very frustrated or angry.
-   When I feel that someone is not listening to me.
-   When I strongly disagree with someone and lose my patience.
-   When I speak too loudly or sound rude during an argument.
-   When I focus on proving that I am right instead of understanding the
    other person.

### Question 7: When do you switch into Passive-Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

-   When I am angry but do not say it directly.
-   When I use sarcasm instead of clearly explaining my problem.
-   When I give taunts because I feel ignored.
-   When I give someone the silent treatment instead of discussing the
    issue.
-   When I complain about someone to others instead of talking to them
    directly.

## 5. Assertive Communication

### Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive?

-   Say what I think clearly and respectfully.
-   Express my needs without blaming the other person.
-   Use "I" statements instead of accusations.
-   Stay calm even when I disagree.
-   Listen to the other person's point of view.
-   Say "no" politely when I cannot do something.
-   Maintain confident but respectful body language.
-   Focus on solving the problem, not winning the argument.
-   Give honest feedback without insulting or judging anyone.
-   Think before speaking, especially when I am angry.
